## Project Overview

This dissertation studies how immigrants become politically integrated in receiving societies and how that process is shaped by everyday intergroup relations. Rather than treating integration as a purely individual outcome, the project emphasizes the relational context in which immigrants navigate political life: contact with natives, local climates of inclusion and exclusion, and broader political debates over immigration.

Across chapters, I ask how interpersonal interactions affect immigrants' sense of belonging, political engagement, and responsiveness to political discourse. The dissertation brings together insights from comparative political behavior, political psychology, and identity politics to explain why social contact can deepen political incorporation in some settings but not in others.

## Central Questions

* When does contact with natives foster immigrants' political integration?
* How does the quality of interpersonal interaction matter, beyond whether contact simply occurs?
* How do anti-immigrant contexts condition immigrants' political attitudes, behavior, and attachment to the host society?

## Research Design

The dissertation focuses on Belgium and Germany and uses a mixed-methods research design. It combines cross-national survey analysis, original qualitative fieldwork, and an experimental chapter to study the political consequences of intergroup relations from multiple angles.

In spring 2025, I conducted dissertation fieldwork in both countries and interviewed first-generation immigrants about their everyday lives, social relationships, and political incorporation. The project also draws on large-scale survey data and develops an experimental design that evaluates how the quality of interpersonal interaction shapes political integration.

## Dissertation Papers

### Included but Not Integrated?

This dissertation paper examines whether contact with natives contributes to immigrants' political integration. It asks whether immigrants can be socially included in everyday life while still remaining politically distant from the host society.

### When Contact Matters

This chapter studies how the quality of interpersonal interaction influences immigrant political integration. Rather than focusing only on the presence of contact, it considers whether positive, meaningful, and trust-building interactions shape belonging and political incorporation differently.

### Qualitative Fieldwork in Belgium and Germany

The qualitative component of the dissertation explores how first-generation immigrants describe their social relationships, political experiences, and sense of place in the host society. These interviews provide context for the survey and experimental findings and help identify the mechanisms through which intergroup relations matter.
